Sharing a screen sends everybody a re-encoded copy of one person's picture, at
whatever quality the call allows. [Frame Player](/) does something else: every player
in the room plays the film itself, and what the room shares is the timeline — where
playback is, whether it is paused, how fast it runs and which file is on. Each
screen shows the film at the quality of its own copy, and the room keeps every
one of them on the same frame.

## Starting a room

1. Open **Watch together…** in the player's menu and choose **Create a room**.
2. Send the code to whoever is joining, or copy the link. The link opens a page
   with a button that starts Frame Player straight into the room, and tells
   anybody who does not have it yet where to get it.
3. They enter the code in the same window, or click the link. For a torrent or a
   link their player opens what you are watching by itself; for a file on your
   disk they open their own copy. Either way they land on the same frame.

From then on a pause, a seek, a change of speed or a jump to the next episode on
any machine happens on all of them. The host can make the room follow only them
with **Only the host controls playback**.

## What is shared, and what stays yours

Position, pause, speed and the file that is playing are the room's. Your volume,
your window and the look of your subtitles stay yours. Two things sit in between,
and the host decides them for the room:

- **The audio track is shared by default.** A room is watching one film, and hearing
  a different soundtrack from the person next to you is a strange way to do it.
- **Subtitles are not shared by default.** One person needs them and another does
  not; sharing the choice would mean switching them off for somebody who cannot
  follow the film without them.

Tracks travel as a description — language, title, codec — rather than as a track
number, because the Russian dub that is track 2 in one copy is often track 3 in
another.

## What each person needs

How easy joining is depends on where the film comes from.

- **A torrent is the best case.** The room shares the torrent's identity, and
  every guest's player connects to it by itself — nobody pastes a magnet — and
  fetches the file from the same swarm. It does not have to finish first: a room
  can watch a torrent that is still downloading, each person at the full quality
  of the release, with every player fetching the part the room is about to watch.
  A guest who joins in the middle of a season is watching within moments, and
  moving to the next episode moves everybody.
- **A link** works when each person's player can open it; pages that need yt-dlp
  are resolved on each machine separately.
- **A file on your disk cannot be sent.** Each person opens their own copy. The
  player compares a fingerprint of the file and its length and says which of three
  things it is: **the same release**, **a different rip** of the same film (right
  length, different file — close, but a few frames can differ), or **a different
  file**. None of them is refused; a guest watching a different cut on purpose is
  told, not stopped.

## How it stays in sync

Keeping two players together is mostly about not making things worse.

**Small differences are corrected with speed, not with a jump.** Jumping to fix a
third of a second means decoding from the previous keyframe, which can stall a
4K file for two seconds and leave it further out than it started. Playing at 103 %
for ten seconds fixes the same difference with nothing to see or hear, since mpv
keeps the pitch where it was. Only a difference of more than two seconds is fixed
with a seek.

**The room waits for whoever is buffering.** Everybody pauses at the same point
and playback resumes by itself once they are ready. A pause somebody asked for is
never lifted this way, and a player that has gone quiet is not allowed to hold the
room for more than 45 seconds.

<Note>
  **What the room cannot fix is your speakers.** Bluetooth headphones add a fifth
  of a second or so of their own on one side of the room, and nothing a player
  sends over the network can see it. If one person hears the film late, that is
  usually why.
</Note>

## Compared with the usual ways

| Compared | [Frame Player](/) | Syncplay | Teleparty | Screen sharing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What you can watch | Your own files, links and torrents | Your own files | Films on Netflix, Disney+ and other services | Anything on one screen |
| Each person needs | Frame Player and a way to play the film | Syncplay, a supported player and their own copy | A browser extension and their own subscription | The call app |
| Picture | Each person's own copy, full quality | Each person's own copy | The service's stream | A re-encoded stream of one screen |
| A torrent | Everyone is connected to it automatically — nobody pastes a magnet | Each person finds and downloads it | — | Only the sharer needs it |
| Still downloading | Watch together while it downloads, each at full quality | Each person downloads it first | — | The sharer's screen, re-encoded |

[Syncplay](https://syncplay.pl) deserves a word: it has done this for mpv, VLC,
MPC-HC and IINA for years and it is very good at it. The difference is that Frame
Player has it built in, and that for a torrent or a link it opens the film for each
guest instead of expecting everyone to have it already.

## What the room server keeps

The room server exists to pass a timeline between players. It knows the room
code, the names people chose to show, and what the room is watching; a file from
a folder you marked private is shared as "hidden", so the timeline syncs and its
name never leaves your computer. There are no accounts and no chat, nothing is
written to disk, and a room is forgotten five minutes after the last person
leaves. The server is open source, and if you would rather run your own, its
address is a setting.
