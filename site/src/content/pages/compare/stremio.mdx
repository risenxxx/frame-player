  - q: Does Frame Player need add-ons like Stremio?
    a: "No. Everything is part of the player — torrent streaming, the catalog, subtitle search, casting and watching together.   - q: Do I need an account?
    a: Not in Frame Player — there is no account at all, and the player reports one thing, once, that it was installed. Stremio has a guest mode that needs no account; its own site says guest mode does not sync your library between devices, which is what the account is for.
  - q: Can Frame Player play files that are already on my computer?

[Stremio](https://www.stremio.com/) is what a lot of people install to watch a
film from a torrent without downloading it first. It describes itself as a
modern media center, and the shape of it is the add-on: you install add-ons, and
they supply the catalogs you browse and the streams you play — Stremio says it
provides no content itself. [Frame Player](/) gets to a similar place from the
other end. It is a video player for Windows and macOS, built on mpv, that plays
any file you give it — and it streams torrents, browses a catalog of films and
series, and sends them to the television, all without anything to install on
top.

## What is the same

**The engine on the desktop.** Stremio's current Windows shell is
[open source](https://github.com/Stremio/stremio-shell-ng) and plays video
through mpv, which is the engine Frame Player is built on. Format support,
hardware decoding and HDR come from the same place.

**Watching a torrent before it has arrived.** Both play a magnet link or a
`.torrent` file seconds after it is opened, without waiting for the whole file.

**Posters, seasons and a "continue watching" row.** Both start from a catalog of
films and series rather than a list of file names, and both remember where you
stopped.

## Where Frame Player goes further

**It is a player for the files you already have.** Frame Player does not need a
film to be in a catalog to play it. Double-click a file, drop a folder, paste a
link — the episode opens, the rest of the folder becomes the queue in natural
order (episode 2 before episode 10), and it resumes where you left it with the
dub and the subtitles you picked, remembered by their language rather than their
track number. The torrents and the catalog are things the player can also do,
not the reason it exists.

**One place for releases, and it is yours.** The catalog takes posters, localized
titles and seasons from TMDB through the project's own proxy, so the player
carries no API key, and the release list for a film comes from a Torznab server
you run — Jackett or Prowlarr. The player ships with no source and suggests none.
Releases are sorted by quality, then dynamic range, then seeders, and the one you
pick opens as a torrent, with resume and the season as a queue.

**Torrent streaming that says what it is doing.** The pieces just ahead of the
playhead are fetched first and nothing is fetched speculatively — opening episode
three of a season torrent downloads episode three. The parts already on disk are
shaded on the seekbar, the readout tells "no peers" from "slow peers", and
uploading is off by default. When a network gets in the way, connection
encryption gets past filters that recognize BitTorrent, a SOCKS5 proxy gets past
blocking by destination, and **Torrent route** can send the swarm out through a
chosen network interface ([how it works](/torrent-streaming)).

**A television without an app on it.** Besides Chromecast, Frame Player sends a
film to a DLNA television's own built-in player, and from a Mac to an AirPlay
receiver. Before you choose a set, each one says whether this particular file
will play on it as it is, needs a quick repack, or cannot play — and the picture
is never re-encoded ([how casting works](/cast-to-tv)).

**Watching together is part of the player.** A six-character code keeps friends
in other places on the same frame. A torrent is picked up by everybody's player
automatically, and each person watches their own copy at full quality — the room
carries the timeline and nothing else ([how it works](/watch-together)).

**The rest of a good player.** Seekbar previews that sharpen to the exact frame,
subtitles searched by the file's own fingerprint, skip intro from the file's
chapters, frame steps in both directions, black bars cropped away, neural-network
upscaling, and a mini player that stays on top.

**No account, and one feature that talks about what you watch.** Frame Player
has no sign-in. The catalog is the one feature that sends what you search for —
to the metadata proxy and to your own Torznab server — so it has an off switch in
the settings, and everything else works without it.

## Where Stremio is the better choice

<Note>
  **If you want the same library on your phone, your TV and your computer**, use
  Stremio. Frame Player runs on Windows and the Mac only.
</Note>

- **Every screen.** Stremio has apps for Android, Android TV, several smart-TV
  platforms, Linux and the web, alongside Windows and macOS. Frame Player reaches a
  television only by casting from a computer.
- **A synced library.** With an account, Stremio keeps your library and your
  progress across all your devices. Frame Player keeps both on the one computer.
- **The add-on ecosystem.** Add-ons can bring in sources Frame Player has no
  equivalent for — community add-ons connect debrid services, and Stremio 5 on
  the desktop added Usenet, FTP and videos inside archives as stream sources.
- **Nothing to set up for releases.** Many people install a ready-made add-on
  and are watching in minutes. Frame Player's catalog needs a Torznab server you
  run yourself before it can list releases.
- **Intel Macs and Linux.** Frame Player's macOS build is for Apple Silicon, and
  there is no Linux build.

Many people would use both: Stremio on the television and the phone, and Frame
Player on the computer, for the files that are already there and the torrents
you open on it.
