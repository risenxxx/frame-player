faq:
  - q: Is there a PotPlayer for Mac?
    a: No. PotPlayer's official site offers Windows installers only — 32-bit, 64-bit and, since August 2026, ARM64 — for Windows 7 to 11. There is no macOS, Linux or mobile version, and running it on a Mac means a Windows virtual machine.
  - q: Is Frame Player free, like PotPlayer?
    a: Yes, and it is also open source, under the GPL-3.0, with its code public. PotPlayer is free to use but its source is not published. Frame Player has no account, no subscription and no advertising; the one thing it reports, once, is that it was installed.
  - q: Can Frame Player use madVR?
    a: No. madVR is a DirectShow renderer for Windows, and Frame Player's picture comes from mpv's own renderer, libplacebo, on both systems. For enlarging a low-resolution video it has neural-network upscalers of its own, which run on a Mac as well as on Windows.
  - q: Does Frame Player step frame by frame, and backwards too?
    a: Yes, forwards with the period key and backwards with the comma. Stepping back is the hard direction for any player, because a video is stored as groups of frames that decode forwards; Frame Player switches to backward decoding on the files where stepping back would otherwise be slow.
  - q: Will Frame Player read my PotPlayer settings or playlists?
    a: No. Settings, hotkeys and bookmarks do not carry over. Frame Player's hotkeys are rebindable, J, K and L work as they do in most players, and opening one episode puts the rest of the folder in the queue by itself.
  - q: Does Frame Player run on Windows on ARM or an Intel Mac?
    a: Not at the moment. It runs on Windows 10 and 11 on Intel and AMD processors, and on Macs with Apple Silicon. On a Windows ARM laptop PotPlayer has a build; on an Intel Mac, IINA or VLC is the one to use.

PotPlayer has a loyal following on Windows, and it has earned it: a player that
starts quickly, plays nearly anything without a codec pack and has a setting for
everything somebody has ever wanted to change. The trouble starts when you get a
Mac, or use one at work, because PotPlayer has never been made for one. The
usual suggestions are a different player on each system, or one with half of
what you were used to. [Frame Player](/) is built to be the same player on
both — the same features, the same shortcuts and the same settings on Windows
and on macOS.

## What is the same

**It plays what you throw at it.** Both players carry their own decoders, so
MKV, HEVC, AV1, 10-bit and HDR files play without installing anything, with
hardware decoding where the graphics card has it.

**The everyday tools.** Playback speed, an A–B loop, frame-by-frame stepping,
audio and subtitle delay, picking the output device, volume above 100 %, saving
the current frame — the things a PotPlayer user reaches for daily are all here,
each on a key you can rebind.

**Free.** Neither costs anything, and neither needs an account.

## Where Frame Player goes further

**It is on the Mac.** The point of this page, but worth spelling out: the same
player, with the same shortcuts, on a Windows PC and on a Mac with Apple Silicon.
A file you stopped in halfway on one machine is a file you know how to drive on
the other.

**An interface you do not have to configure first.** PotPlayer's strength is a
preferences window with a setting for everything, and the price is finding the
one you need. Frame Player keeps what you use on the video and in a context
menu, and the rest in one settings sheet. Underneath, it still reads an mpv.conf,
so an option the interface does not offer can be written by hand.

**Stepping back, made fast.** Any player can step forwards; stepping back means
decoding a whole group of frames to show the one before. Frame Player measures
the file and, where that would be slow, decodes it backwards instead — so
holding the key walks back through a scene rather than stalling on each frame.
[How it works](/guides/step-back-one-frame).

**The picture, cropped and enlarged without a renderer to set up.** Auto crop
measures frames across the whole file and removes the black bars baked into the
video, each episode measured on its own ([how](/guides/remove-black-bars)).
Upscaling uses neural-network shaders made for live action or for animation, and
the panel says when the window is too small for them to make any difference
([how](/upscaling)). Both work the same on a Mac, where there is no madVR.

**Subtitles matched to your release.** The search goes by the file's own
fingerprint first, which finds subtitles made for exactly that copy, and a
subtitle made for a different frame rate is flagged before you download it —
that is the one that [drifts further out of sync](/guides/subtitles-out-of-sync)
the longer you watch.

**Torrents, casting and watching together.** A magnet link plays while it
downloads ([how](/torrent-streaming)); a film goes to a Chromecast or a DLNA
television without being re-encoded, and from a Mac to an AirPlay one
([how](/cast-to-tv)); and a six-character code keeps friends in other places on
the same frame ([how](/watch-together)). None of the three is built into
PotPlayer.

## Where PotPlayer is the better choice

<Note>
  **On a Windows ARM laptop**, PotPlayer has a native build and Frame Player does
  not.
</Note>

- **Depth of configuration.** If you want to choose the decoder per codec, chain
  video filters or tune the renderer in detail, PotPlayer exposes far more of
  that than Frame Player ever will.
- **madVR and the MPC Video Renderer.** Some viewers have settled on madVR's
  scaling and tone mapping and want nothing else; PotPlayer can use it, and
  Frame Player cannot.
- **Subtitles from speech.** PotPlayer can generate subtitles from the audio with
  Whisper, even while a live stream plays, and translate subtitles online. Frame
  Player finds subtitles but does not make or translate them.
- **More than files.** 3D output for 3D televisions and glasses, 360° video, TV
  tuners and capture cards, and recording video, streams, the screen or a game.
  Frame Player plays files and links, and does none of that.
- **Older Windows.** PotPlayer still installs on Windows 7 and 8.1; Frame Player
  needs Windows 10 or 11.

## And the other Mac players

If the Mac is all you need a player for, there are good ones that are not
cross-platform. **IINA** is a native Mac front end on the same mpv engine as Frame
Player, and the one to use on an Intel Mac ([compared](/compare/iina)). **VLC**
runs everywhere and plays nearly anything ([compared](/compare/vlc)). **Infuse**
is a polished player built around a library of posters, with a paid Pro tier
([compared](/compare/infuse)). IINA and Infuse stop at the Mac, so neither can
be the player on the Windows machine next to it — which is the gap Frame Player
fills.
