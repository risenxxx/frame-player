Ordinary scaling, however good, can only interpolate: it decides what goes between
two pixels from the pixels around them, which is why an enlarged 480p picture
reads as soft rather than blocky. A neural network trained on pairs of small and
large pictures can do better — it has learned what an edge, a line of an ink
drawing or the grain of a wall tends to look like, and puts it back. In mpv that
has long been possible with shaders such as Anime4K, at the price of downloading
them, writing them into a config file and binding keys to switch them.
[Frame Player](/) ships three of them and puts them in a menu.

## How to turn it on

1. Right-click the picture and open **Picture**, or press **C**.
2. Under **Upscaling**, choose **Video**, **Anime** or **Strong**. **Off** is the
   default.
3. Choose whether the choice holds for **This file** or for the **Playlist** — the
   folder the file is in, or the torrent it came from.

There is also a shortcut, **Upscaling: next mode**, which steps through the four;
it has no key until you give it one in the shortcut editor.

## The three modes

| Mode | Network | Made for | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Video** | ArtCNN C4F16 DS | Films, series, filmed video | Enlarges ×2, removing noise and sharpening as it goes |
| **Anime** | Anime4K Upscale CNN x2 M | Drawn animation | Enlarges ×2, tuned for line art and flat color |
| **Strong** | Anime4K Upscale GAN x2 M | Sources up to 720p | Draws texture in across the whole frame |

**Video and Anime reconstruct; Strong invents.** The first two put back edges that
are there in the picture, only blurred. Strong is a generative network: it adds
texture where the source has none, and that is what makes a whole frame read as
detailed rather than just its outlines. It pays for that twice. It was trained on
animation, so on live action faces can pick up artifacts. And the texture it
invents is not quite the same from one frame to the next, so a still scene
shimmers. We measured that on two consecutive frames of a 480p film, as the
difference between them in PSNR — the lower, the more the two frames differ:

| Two consecutive frames, 480p film | PSNR between them |
|---|---|
| Ordinary scaling | 35.4 dB |
| Video (ArtCNN) | 34.6 dB |
| Strong (Anime4K GAN) | 33.9 dB |
| A restoring GAN in front of Strong (not shipped) | 31.3 dB |

The frames differ to begin with, because things move; what the table shows is how
much each network adds on top of that. That is why Strong is meant for small,
soft sources, where it has the most to give. **On anything larger than 720p** —
by either its long or its short side — it works as Video, and the panel says
"Above 720p this works as “Video”". A playlist set to Strong can therefore mix
720p and 1080p episodes without either losing the upscaler or dropping frames.

## Why these three, and not the bigger ones

Each family comes in several sizes, and the larger ones are not a sharper switch
— they are a slower one. We measured the GPU time per frame on an M3 Max, drawing
into a 3400×2000 window:

| GPU time per frame | 720p source | 1080p source |
|---|---|---|
| No shader | 2.3 ms | 2.9 ms |
| **Video** — ArtCNN C4F16 DS | 7.2 ms | 8.5 ms |
| **Anime** — Anime4K CNN x2 M | 3.9 ms | 6.4 ms |
| **Strong** — Anime4K GAN x2 M | 9.9 ms | 20.7 ms |
| Anime4K CNN x2 L | | 8.6 ms |
| Anime4K CNN x2 VL | | 15 ms |
| ArtCNN C4F32 | | 37 ms |
| Anime4K GAN x4 UL | | 39 ms |
| Anime4K Restore GAN UL + GAN x2 M | | 44 ms |

<p class="table-note">GPU time per frame, the sum of the render passes mpv reports, with mpv 0.41.</p>

A 24 fps film has 42 ms for each frame and a 60 fps one 17 ms. The heaviest
variants take most or all of that on the fastest Mac there is, and an integrated
graphics chip has a fraction of its cores — so the player offers the variant of
each family that leaves room to spare, and does not offer the rest.

## It runs only when the picture is enlarged

Each shader decides for itself, on every frame, whether to run: only when the
picture on screen is more than 1.3 times its own size in both directions (1.2 for
the two Anime4K modes). So with upscaling on:

- a **720p episode in full screen** on a 1080p display is 1.5 times its size, and
  is upscaled;
- a **1080p film** on a 1080p display is drawn at its own size, and is left alone;
- **anything 4K** is left alone on practically any screen;
- **a small window** may not be enlarged enough — the same file starts being
  upscaled when the window grows or goes full screen.

Sizes are counted in the screen's real pixels, so on a Retina display a window
holds twice as many in each direction as it seems to. Cropping the black bars
away or zooming in does not count as enlarging: the shaders compare the whole
decoded frame with the part of the picture inside the window.

A switch that is on and doing nothing would look broken, so the panel says which
it is: **"Active: the picture is 1.6× its own size"**, **"Not active now: the
picture is barely enlarged"**, or **"Not needed: the video is no smaller than the
screen"**.

## This file, or the whole season

Whether a picture is worth enlarging is a property of the video — a season ripped
from DVD is soft in every episode — so the choice is kept per file or per
playlist, and the file's own choice wins. Set the playlist to Anime and every
episode in the folder or torrent opens with it, including ones you have not
watched yet; set one episode back to Off and only that one is. A link has no
playlist, so for a link the choice is the file's.

There is deliberately no setting for every file. A network left on everywhere
would run over every 4K film it could not improve, and on a laptop you would pay
for that on battery.

## The first time on Windows

A shader has to be compiled for the graphics card before it can run, and mpv
does that the first time the picture is large enough for it. On a Mac that takes
about a quarter of a second — we measured 0.27 s and three dropped frames — the
same for every mode.

On Windows the Video mode is the expensive one. Its passes are compiled by
Microsoft's shader compiler for Direct3D 11, which took over twelve seconds for
ArtCNN on a current desktop graphics card, against a tenth of a second for Anime
and a third of a second for Strong. Two things keep that from mattering more than
once:

- **It happens once per computer.** The compiled shaders are kept between
  sessions, so the next time the player starts, upscaling begins at once.
- **The player says so.** While the first compile runs the picture stands still,
  so the player dims it and shows "Preparing upscaling — Only the first time on
  this computer" with a running clock, instead of looking frozen.

<Note>
  The compile usually lands at the moment the picture first grows past the
  threshold — while you drag the window larger or switch to full screen — because
  that is when the shader is first needed.
</Note>

## Compared with setting glsl-shaders in mpv by hand

If you have used Anime4K with mpv, you know the routine: download the shaders,
list them in `glsl-shaders` in mpv.conf or bind modes to keys in input.conf, and
remember which one suits which file. Frame Player does the same thing underneath
— the same mpv, the same shader files, unmodified — and changes four things:

- **Nothing to download or write.** The shaders ship inside the player.
- **The choice is remembered** per file or per playlist rather than being one
  global line, so a live-action film after an anime season does not inherit the
  anime shader.
- **The panel says whether it is running**, and why not.
- **The compiled shaders are cached** between sessions. mpv does that by default
  on its own; the library form of mpv that players are built on does not, which is
  why the first Windows release of this feature compiled again in every session.

If you prefer your own chain, keep it: when your mpv.conf sets `glsl-shaders`,
the player sends none of its own and the panel says "Shaders are set in mpv.conf,
so the player leaves them alone".

## Where it does not apply

- **Casting.** The television decodes the video itself, so nothing the computer's
  GPU does can reach it; the picture settings are unavailable while casting.
- **Watching together.** The choice is your own and is not shared with the room;
  everyone picks what suits their screen.

alongside its other third-party notices.
