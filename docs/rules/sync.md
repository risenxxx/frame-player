# Watching together

One shared timeline over a small relay: the wire, the reconciler, readiness and what a room may know.

Part of the rule book: [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md) carries the one-line
version of each rule here, this file carries the whole of it — the identifiers,
the measurements and the failure the rule was written against.
The *why* behind it — what was tried, what was measured — is in
[`../watch-together.md`](../watch-together.md).

- **Watching together is one shared timeline and nothing else, and the shape of the wire is the whole design.** What travels is a **snapshot of the state after a change** — `{content, paused, position, speed, at, rev, by}` — never the gesture. A replayed action is not idempotent, does not survive a dropped message, and cannot answer the only question a lagging or late-joining viewer has, which is where they should be *now*; a snapshot answers all three, which is why every guard in the reconciler can simply `return`: a moment when this player must not be touched is a moment it will be corrected a second later. `at` is the **relay's** clock and `rev` is the relay's to assign — a client that fills them in is overwritten, which is what keeps revisions monotonic when two people press space at once, and what lets each peer know only its own offset from one clock instead of everybody agreeing about wall time. **Ordering is by `rev`, never by `at`**: the relay is the only participant with one opinion about what happened first, while `at` is a clock *estimate* whose error bars are half a round trip — two clients ordering by timestamp could disagree about which change came last and both be right. So `at` answers "where should playback be" and `rev` answers "which of these do I believe". `shouldApply` is the guard, and two things in it look like mistakes and are not: **`>=` rather than `>`** (a refusal is answered by re-sending the *same* revision, and that message is the correction that pulls a guest back from where they optimistically moved — dropping it strands them there), and **a `welcome` applies unconditionally** (it describes the room as it is, and covers a room re-created from zero). Most of the ordering is not ours anyway: a WebSocket is TCP, so within one connection messages cannot arrive out of order at all, and the `socket !== sock` guards drop a previous socket's traffic after a reconnect. **Delay costs nothing** — a snapshot that arrives two seconds late still projects the right position, which is the deepest reason the wire carries state rather than actions. **Tracks are the one place the roadmap's "sync the timeline, not the presentation" is deliberately crossed**, and it is crossed by **a rule of the room rather than a preference of a viewer**: `shareAudio`/`shareSubs` live on the relay beside `hostOnly`, are set by the host in the room panel, and arrive in `welcome` and every `members` broadcast. All three are the host's for one reason — the host owns the room's rules, and a panel where one switch answers to a different person than the two beside it is unpredictable. Defaults are **audio on** (a room listens to one soundtrack) and **subtitles off** (one viewer needing them and another not is the ordinary case, and sharing the choice would turn them off for somebody who cannot follow the film). A per-viewer switch was built first and was wrong: a room where one person's audio choice reaches everybody and another's does not is a room whose own members disagree about what it does. They ride on the timeline (`tracks`) rather than in a message of their own, which gets last-writer-wins and handshake delivery for free; the cost is that **a publish must merge with what the room already holds**, since a snapshot carrying `{audio}` alone says "no subtitle preference" and would wipe the other kind for everybody who shares it. They travel as a **description resolved by `matchTrack`**, never an id: ids are positions inside one file, so the dub that is #2 in one rip is #3 in another. **Drift is corrected by bending the speed, never by seeking** (the roadmap decided this in advance and it is right): an exact seek costs decoding from the preceding keyframe — 1.9 s on a 4K60 VP9 file — so seeking to fix 300 ms stops for two seconds to correct a third of one and lands further out than it started, while ten seconds at 103 % fixes it inaudibly (`scaletempo2` preserves pitch). Three bands in `drift.ts`: inside the deadband nothing, ≤2 s a proportional nudge capped at ±10 % aiming to erase it over ten seconds, >2 s one seek. **The deadband is measured rather than chosen** (`deadbandFor`): twice the clock uncertainty, clamped to 40–150 ms — a flat 150 ms is what a viewer felt as a room that held to a tenth of a second and then stopped trying, and a tenth of a second is a guess about the network rather than a property of it. **And the local position must come from `positionNow()`, not the `time-pos` mirror**: the mirror is an event, so reading it is tens of milliseconds late — a systematic error in the one measurement whose job is to be small, and it sat inside the old band where nothing could see it — and while *paused* there is no speed to bend, so the tolerance drops to 0.25 s and it seeks. The correction is applied around the **room's** speed rather than around 1, or a room watching at 1.5× would silently lose it, and it is written straight to mpv rather than through the verb, because it is this machine catching up and publishing it would have every peer chasing every other peer. **Readiness is the relay's decision and it freezes the room with a real timeline** rather than a flag beside one — a paused snapshot at the position playback had reached — so every client's projection keeps working unmodified and somebody joining mid-wait sees the same state as everybody else. Three rules make that bearable: a pause a *person* asked for is never lifted by the relay, resuming while somebody loads means "resume when they are done", and a member who never reports counts as ready after 45 s, or one player that died mid-buffer holds an evening. **What a client reports is `ready.ts`, a pure predicate with tests of its own, because both ways of being wrong are invisible from outside and both had shipped at once.** "A file is open" is not "playback began": `filename` and `path` are set the moment a load is *issued*, so a torrent whose first byte never arrives satisfies `player.hasFile` for ever — the guest sat in front of a black window announcing themselves ready while the room played on without them. `playback-restart` is the event that means frames are being produced, and `startedSrc` is where it is recorded. In the other direction, a **definitive failure has to stop holding the room**: a magnet that timed out will not resolve on the next tick either, so a guest whose swarm never answered went on reporting "buffering" for the rest of the session, bounded only by that 45 s grace. Two things next to it fail the same silent way. `openContent` must leave `sync.opening` false on **every** path — its early returns did not, so a flag raised by a run that has since gone stale stood for the rest of the session, which is reachable by doing nothing wrong (resolve a magnet, have the host switch to a local file) and shows as a permanent "opening what the room is watching". And **the room's own open is the one wait in the player with nothing on screen at all**, because `beforeLoad` has not run: no `opening.busy`, and no torrent status either, since that poll hangs off `player.filePath` — a chip line for up to 90 s, reported as a player that does nothing. It now raises the ordinary loading overlay, with an **elapsed clock** rather than a figure, because a resolve is the one wait with nothing to count; covering a *playing* picture is still refused, the trade the casting screen already makes. The reason for a failure reaches the panel too (`sync.failedReason`, through the `torrentFailureText` the link box shows for the same call) instead of `console.warn`: "nobody is seeding this" and "the player is broken" are the two readings a viewer is otherwise left choosing between. **What a member is doing travels beside whether they are ready**: `readinessOf` answers both at once, and the relay stores the word without looking at it — the `ContentRef` rule again, so a new one is a frontend change and never a redeploy — and hands it back in `members`. That is what lets a room tell "still looking for the torrent" from "downloading it" from "gave up on it", which had all been one word on everybody else's screen. It is optional on the wire and an unknown value falls back to that word, because a room whose members run different builds is the ordinary case rather than an error. Two verdicts travel while *ready*, which is not a contradiction: "could not open it" and "cannot open it at all" are precisely what stop the room waiting, and they are what somebody needs telling. And **nothing retries a failed open by itself** — the room re-states its content with every pause and every seek, so retrying on arrival would be a magnet resolve every few seconds for the length of the film — which left leaving the room and rejoining as the only way back in, hence the button on the panel's failure line. Finally, the magnet a room hands over **carries the torrent's own trackers** (`trackers_of`, capped at six): locally a magnet reopens against the metadata cached beside the data and needs none, but where nothing was remembered — a `.torrent` file, or history switched off — the fallback was a bare info hash, i.e. a DHT-only magnet, which on a network that filters UDP is exactly the ninety seconds of nothing this passage is about. They are opt-in on `magnetFor` for a reason of the same shape: every caller that *stores* a magnet passes none, so a magnet built today stays byte-identical to one built last year and the recents list does not grow a second row for one torrent. Joining is itself not-ready, which is correct: the newcomer has a file to open. **The bus is the existing verbs**, which is the whole of "new features are shared by design": every button, menu row, hotkey and gesture already routes through `playback.svelte.ts`, so publishing from there covers a control added next year — and `SYNC_BEHAVIOR`, a `Record<ActionId, 'shared'|'personal'|'solo'>`, makes a new action a **compile error** until somebody classifies it, exactly as `CAST_BEHAVIOR` does and for the same failure (every casting bug that module exists for was an omission). Only the A–B loop is `solo`; frame stepping is `personal` because a frame is an order of magnitude below the deadband, and repeat mode is `personal` because a viewer whose file loops while the room moves on ends up on different content, which heals itself when the room's content is opened. Gestures publish **on release only** (`onSeekUp`, `endScrub` — the same rule the cast seekbar keeps), because a drag is a stream of positions and each would be a seek on somebody else's machine. **An arriving timeline is not a gesture**: it goes to mpv directly rather than through the verbs, or the OSD, the drag guard and — fatally — the publish would all fire, and every peer would re-broadcast every peer. **A torrent is the case this is good at and the reason the roadmap paired the two**: an info hash plus an index names a file exactly, the metadata is already cached beside the data, so a guest joining mid-season is a `torrent_add` of a few milliseconds and switching episode is the same hash with another index. A **local file cannot be sent**, so what travels is enough to recognise a copy — the OpenSubtitles release hash (reused from `opensubtitles.rs`, not rewritten), the duration and the size — and the panel says *the same release* / *a different rip* / *a different file* rather than refusing: a viewer who deliberately opened the director's cut is making a mistake worth mentioning, not correcting. Comparison is **identity, not equality** (a late title, a firmed-up duration or a magnet rebuilt from the hash must not read as a different film, or playback restarts for everybody). **The room code is Crockford's base32**: I, L, O and U are out of the alphabet *and* the ones people type anyway are folded onto their look-alikes rather than refused — an alphabet that merely excluded them would reject the code the viewer is looking at. `frameplayer://join/<code>` is **offered, never obeyed** — a custom scheme is a surface any page can aim at, and exactly one shape is parsed; anything else is ignored in silence. The relay is `relay.frameplayer.app` by default (`DEFAULT_RELAY` in `wire.svelte.ts`), and the address is a **field in the settings sheet** (Основные) rather than a build-time constant — self-hosting `services/relay/` is a setting, not a fork, and an empty field means the default rather than no relay. It is not in the room dialog, where it started: almost nobody runs their own, so it was a question with one answer in front of the two controls that matter. `socketUrl` **refuses plaintext off loopback**: the relay carries what everybody in the room is watching, and over `ws://` that failure would be invisible because it works. The relay never parses content (opaque JSON, size-bounded), so a new kind of source is a frontend change and never a redeploy, and `shared/sync-protocol.txt` is what keeps the two languages' field names in step — with the trap that **`go test` caches and the fixture lives outside the Go module**, hence the `t.Setenv` at the top of that test.
