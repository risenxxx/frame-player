# CSS: the box model, the cascade and the things that are silently wrong

The reset every size written down depends on, specificity between borrowed classes, and the handful of rendering traps measured in both engines.

Part of the rule book: [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md) carries the one-line
version of each rule here, this file carries the whole of it — the identifiers,
the measurements and the failure the rule was written against.

- **A bar's gradient tail must not be part of its hit area.** `.topbar` and `.osc` extend well past their controls to fade the scrim (76 px vs 48 px of content; 44 px of top padding on the OSC). While that padding belonged to the element, clicks in it were swallowed by the bar — the user is visibly clicking video and the player ignores them. The gradients live in `::before` with `pointer-events: none`, and the elements themselves are sized to their content. **An absolutely-positioned `::before` paints *above* in-flow children**, so `.osc::before` needs `z-index: -1` plus `isolation: isolate` on `.osc` — without it the gradient covered the control row and every button looked disabled. `.topbar` survives without it only because its children are themselves absolutely positioned and come later in DOM order; do not rely on that when adding children. Anything targeting the scrim must therefore target `::before` (e.g. `.player.no-video .topbar::before`), and `data-tauri-drag-region` still needs the bar itself to receive events, which is why `pointer-events: none` on the whole bar is *not* the fix.
- **Focusing an input scrolls it into view, and that silently undoes whatever scrolling you just arranged.** The subtitle panel's sign-in form unfolds at the bottom of a box that already scrolls (`.settings` is `max-height: 100%; overflow-y: auto`), so opening it has to bring the form into view or the button reads as doing nothing. Scrolling there and *then* focusing the first field looks correct and is not: the browser's own scroll-on-focus runs afterwards, is instant where ours is smooth, and lands the field near the top — leaving the form's buttons under the fold, which is the half that mattered. `focus({ preventScroll: true })` is half the fix, and the scroll is aimed at the form with `scrollIntoView({ block: 'end' })` rather than at the container's `scrollHeight`, so it stays correct whatever else the panel is showing. The other half came from a WKWebView harness built against this exact structure, because two plausible causes had to be ruled out rather than argued about: **a nested scroll container is not the problem** (every API — `scrollIntoView`, `scrollTo`, `scrollTop`, smooth or instant — reaches the bottom of a `max-height` box), and **neither is the form being inserted on click** (it works with no waiting at all, `tick` or `requestAnimationFrame` notwithstanding). What does fail is the combination: with a `focus()` call nearby, a **smooth** scroll stops 710 px short of 717 — *and `preventScroll: true` does not save it*. So the scroll is instant, which lands exactly, and runs a second time on the next frame to cover the box still settling as the form unfolds. General rule: when a scroll "does nothing", suspect a focus call before suspecting the scroller.
- **Every box is its border box, and every size written down is an outer size.** The reset (`*, *::before, *::after`) lives in app.css. The project went without it for a long time and the cost was never legible as a box-sizing problem: the symptom is always "something is a pixel or two off", found by measuring, and the cause is always a border or a padding sitting outside the number somebody wrote. Four in one session — the mini player's exit button breaking the concentric-corner arithmetic, the preview's crossfade layer 4px too big over a `height: 100%`, the context menu 14px taller than the cap the placement gave it, and the carousel arrow a 34px circle in a 32px button — each fixed by a local `box-sizing: border-box`, nine of which had accumulated. That is the reset written one element at a time, and only where somebody had already been bitten. The one thing the reset does **not** change is `<button>` and `<select>`: both engines already make those border-box in their own stylesheets (measured in headless Chrome and in a WKWebView harness), which is why all nine local declarations sat on a div, a span, an img or an input, and why a `<button>` with `width: 100%` and padding never overhung its parent. Do not "compensate" a button's size for the reset — it was already outer. Everything else was adjusted so the pixels did not move: 43 of the 44 boxes measured before and after are identical in both engines, and the exception is the hover preview's height, where `aspect-ratio` follows box-sizing and so now shapes the outlined frame rather than the picture inside it (1.75px, documented at the rule). Two latent bugs the reset fixed rather than preserved, both in the settings sheet, both from `100%` meaning "the container plus my own 38px": at full height it stood 32px taller than the backdrop's padded grid area and reached 39px from the top of the window, *under* the 48px title bar the 56px of padding exists to clear; and in a narrow window it escaped the 24px side inset entirely, sitting 5px from each window edge. It now fits exactly, which below ~646px of window width also means it is 38px narrower than it used to be and the tab row scrolls sooner — the row is built to scroll, and the alternative was a dialog that keeps its width by ignoring its own margins.
- **A flex item does not shrink below its content unless told to.** `min-width: auto` is the default, so `overflow: hidden; text-overflow: ellipsis` on a label inside a flex row never gets the chance to fire: the row grows instead, and the panel around it grows a horizontal scrollbar. Every ellipsizing label in a flex row needs `min-width: 0` on itself *and* on each flex ancestor between it and the fixed-width box — in the queue that is the name, the row button and the row. **And all of that is worth nothing if the row is not actually a flex container**, which is how the queue spent a while with names running past the button and under the × with no way to read them. The button carries two classes, `.menu-item` (`display: block`) and `.chapter-item` (`display: flex`); they weigh exactly the same, so the winner was whichever came later in the file — and it was `block`. Measured in the compiled bundle rather than argued about: `display:flex` at byte 27352, `display:block` at 27753. With the parent not a flex container the `flex`/`min-width` pair is inert and the name stays an **inline** span, where `overflow: hidden` does not apply at all. Hence `.menu-item.chapter-item`: a rule that has to beat another is written to win rather than left to source order. Two traps around it worth naming. Reconstructing a panel's CSS by hand to test it is how this was missed twice — the harness had the two rules in the opposite order and "proved" the layout was fine; building the real bundle and feeding *that* into the harness is the only version of this test worth trusting. And the panel is positioned by its right edge with `left: auto`, so it grows leftwards without bound: a long name made it 972px wide inside a 700px window at `offsetLeft: -297`, clipped and unreachable. Menus therefore carry a `max-width` with a viewport term (`min(520px, calc(100vw - 48px))`), which is also what keeps them inside the mini player.
- **Reusing a class means beating it, and equal weight is decided by source order — this has now cost two bugs.** The queue rows lost `display: flex` to `.menu-item`'s `display: block` that way; the torrent list's delete button then lost `position: static` to `.card-forget`'s `position: absolute` the same way, and with no positioned ancestor in the row it flew to `top: 4px; right: 4px` of the overlay and sat under the title-bar logo — invisible as well, since `.card-forget` also carries `opacity: 0` until a `.card` is hovered. Both times the override was written as a bare second class of equal specificity, and both times the borrowed rule happened to sit later in the file. So: **write the winning selector** (`.menu-item.torrent-file`, `.card-forget.torrow-forget`), never a lone modifier class. Measure it in the **built bundle**, not a hand-written copy: `npm run build`, then read the rule offsets out of `build/_app/immutable/assets/*.css` — the compiled order is the thing that decides. The bug reproduces exactly by `sed`-ing the winning selector back down to one class in that same file and re-measuring in a browser, which is the cheapest possible proof that the diagnosis is right rather than plausible. **The same arithmetic decides a control's own hover against its container's**, and there source order does not save you: Svelte scopes a descendant selector as `.a.svelte-x .b:where(.svelte-x)` — the `:where()` is free, so `.row:hover .cross` weighs (0,4,0) against a bare `.cross:hover`'s (0,3,0) and the container wins. A three-strength control (resting / row hovered / itself hovered) therefore needs its top state written as `.row:hover .cross:hover`, or the glyph never reaches full strength under the pointer.
- **Animating `opacity` on a small glyph makes it twitch, and no amount of measuring geometry will find it.** The torrent list's delete cross was reported "jumping and coming back" on hover. Geometry was identical across rest / row-hover / cross-hover — same row height, same offset inside the row, to the hundredth of a pixel, measured both standalone and in the real page nesting with transitions disabled. Nothing in the box model moves. The cause is that taking a layer off full opacity makes the engine re-rasterise it and switch how it antialiases, which on a **1.4px stroke** is visible as a wobble; Chrome does not reproduce it, WKWebView does. So the three strengths (resting / row hovered / hovered) are **color**, and `opacity` is pinned at 1 and never transitioned. Worth knowing generally: opacity is the wrong lever for a control that is always on screen — it is for things that appear and disappear, where the rasterisation switch is hidden by the fade itself.
- **Accent colors**: indigo `#6366f1` (fills) / `#818cf8` (hover/active/focus); white text on accent surfaces. Indigo means **one** thing — selected / on / primary / focused (`.segopt.sel`, `.tab.sel`, `.switch.on`, `.updbtn`, focus rings). Passive readouts stay white, like `.seekfill`: the OSD bar used to be indigo, which inverted the logic — the control you drag was white while the thing that merely reports a value wore the accent. The exception is `.card-progress`, indigo on purpose because it sits over arbitrary video frames where white would disappear. Floating surfaces come from one scale (`rgba(16,16,22,~0.96)`), not per-component values — **and that fill is half of the recipe**: it is also the start screen's own color, so a surface without the hairline border beside it is invisible there, and one without the shadow is lost over a bright video frame. Taking the fill and leaving the rest is the mistake to watch for. **One deliberate exception, and it is a different token rather than a loosening of the rule**: `.btn-catalog` on the start screen wears a 1px *gradient hairline* (`#818cf8` → `#6366f1` → `#a855f7`), which is the only place a second hue appears. A flat indigo fill beside `Открыть файл` would have been two primaries, i.e. none; a gradient ring is not a fill and can carry its own meaning — *this one is worth knowing about* — and the violet end is what makes it legible as a gradient at all, since indigo shading into indigo is invisible at a hairline. It is **an SVG stroke, and the shape it is not is the lesson**. The usual padding-box/border-box double background needs an opaque interior, which this button does not have (transparent fill, changing on hover), so it shipped first as a masked `::before` — a gradient rectangle with its middle punched out by `mask-composite: exclude`. That makes the ring's thickness **the difference between two rectangles rather than a drawn line**, i.e. whatever survives after the outer and inner mask rects have each been snapped to the device-pixel grid *independently*. Exact at ratio 2, which is why macOS never saw it; wrong on Windows, where the button sits at a fractional offset by construction (`.start-actions` centres a wrapping flex row) and 125 %/150 % display scaling adds a fractional ratio on top. Reported from a Windows build and reproduced: **thinner along the bottom, clipped at every corner but the top-left** — the box origin rounds outward and keeps its pixel while the far edges round inward and lose theirs. Measured against the built stylesheet in both engines (headless Chrome at 1/1.25/1.5, a WKWebView harness at 2), integrating the paint over each 8×8 corner quadrant and normalising to one covered pixel of the same line, so a stroke, a mask and a plain CSS border compare directly — ideal is π/2 · 7.5 ≈ 11.8: at **1.25** mask **5.7–8.9** against stroke **10.1–11.1** (border 7.1–7.8), at 1 mask 10.4–11.2 against stroke 10.3–10.4, at 2 mask 11.1–11.3 against stroke 11.7–11.8. The sides say the same: at 1.25 the mask paints its left edge over two whole device pixels and each of the other three over one. What the stroke buys is evenness rather than perfection — four sides within 3 % of each other at every ratio measured, corners within 16 % at the worst of them against the mask's 36 %. Four things the stroke version depends on, none of them optional. **The half pixel has to live in SVG user space, not in the element's position** — a 1px stroke is centred on its path, so the centreline belongs half a pixel inside the box, and writing that as `left: -0.5px` does not survive: Blink snaps a box's paint offset to whole pixels and the line then straddled the padding-box edge as two pixels at 44 % each, dimmer than the border beside it. So the `<svg>` sits on the border box (`inset: -1px`, since insets resolve against the *padding* box — `inset: 0` is a full pixel wrong, measured at +1.0px when this was a `::before`) and the `<rect>` carries `x: 0.5px; width: calc(100% - 1px); rx: 7.5px` — geometry as **CSS** properties because `calc()` is needed and an attribute cannot take it, verified honoured in WKWebView as well as Blink. **`width: auto` on that `<svg>` is not "fill the insets"**: an `<svg>` is a replaced element, so `auto` means its intrinsic 300 × 150 and the over-constrained box drops `right`/`bottom` — measured, the ring's right and bottom edges landed 300px and 150px off the button, with nothing painted within 6px of either. app.css's `svg { width: 14px }` had been masking that, and is also why the size has to be overridden rather than deleted. And the ring's colours are **custom properties read by the `<stop>`s**, so `:hover` brightens one ramp instead of keeping two in step.
